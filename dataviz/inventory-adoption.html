Getting people to actually use the system

We had a chemical inventory system that almost nobody used. Before adding more training, I asked people what was actually getting in the way.

The story

Problem
Chemicals weren't getting logged, labels got skipped, and things weren't where the system said. Honestly, I didn't know where everything was either when I started.
What I did
I learned the system from its owner, then surveyed everyone who used it. 2 answers kept coming up: people didn't know how to use it, and they couldn't find things. My working group fixed that first with one scanning station that has everything in one spot. Then we wrote guides, ran training, and cleaned up storage.
What changed
People were using the station before I left, and the records were audit-ready. The fix turned out to be about putting things in one place, not more training.

# Weekly reminder: chemicals expiring in the next 30 days, grouped by owner.
# Built and tested against the inventory system's API; not launched before I left.
import datetime as dt
from itertools import groupby
import requests

cutoff = dt.date.today() + dt.timedelta(days=30)
items = requests.get(f"{API_BASE}/inventory", headers=AUTH, timeout=30).json()

expiring = sorted(
    (i for i in items
     if i["status"] == "active" and dt.date.fromisoformat(i["expiry_date"]) <= cutoff),
    key=lambda i: i["owner_email"],
)
for owner, group in groupby(expiring, key=lambda i: i["owner_email"]):
    rows = [(i["name"], i["location"], i["expiry_date"]) for i in group]
    send_email(to=owner, subject=f"{len(rows)} chemicals expire within 30 days", rows=rows)
