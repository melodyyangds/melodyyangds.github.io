Keeping maintenance and calibration on time

When preventive maintenance or calibration goes late in a regulated company, the equipment can't be used and it can turn into an audit finding. I owned this for my team, and we had zero late work orders in 6 of 7 years.

The story

Problem
100+ pieces of equipment, all on different schedules. Someone has to know what's due, which months get busy, and what's about to slip.
What I did
I managed PM and calibration in Maximo, first as custodian and then as approver. I tracked due dates, sent reminders early, booked vendors, and found backup approvers when needed. After a reorg, I also led moving about 400 equipment records to the right owners.
What changed
Zero late PM or calibration in 6 of 7 years, and the other year had just one. This page is my take on how that tracking looks as a dashboard.

Queries behind this view · BigQuery-style SQL 
-- Heatmap: work orders due and late, by asset class and month
SELECT
  a.asset_class,
  DATE_TRUNC(w.due_date, MONTH)                                   AS due_month,
  w.work_type,                                                    -- 'PM' or 'CAL'
  COUNT(*)                                                        AS due,
  COUNTIF(w.completed_date > w.due_date
          OR (w.completed_date IS NULL AND w.due_date < CURRENT_DATE())) AS late
FROM work_orders AS w
JOIN assets      AS a USING (asset_id)
WHERE EXTRACT(YEAR FROM w.due_date) = @year
GROUP BY 1, 2, 3;

-- Reorg cleanup: records whose owning org no longer matches the org chart
SELECT a.asset_id, a.owner_org AS system_org, m.new_org, a.custodian_id
FROM assets AS a
JOIN org_mapping AS m ON a.custodian_id = m.employee_id
WHERE a.owner_org IS DISTINCT FROM m.new_org   -- also catches blank owners
ORDER BY m.new_org, a.asset_id;
