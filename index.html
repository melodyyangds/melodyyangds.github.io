Reports that took days now take under an hour

~40% fewer ad hoc requests

Every metrics question meant a manual pull, and the numbers didn't always match.

Power BIDimensional modelingStar schemaDAX / Power QueryKPI standardizationSelf-service reporting

Problem
There was no single source for reporting. Answering a leadership question took 2 to 5 days of manual work, and since every report rebuilt the logic, two people could pull the same metric and get different numbers.
What I did
I built an ETL with auto-refresh over 6K+ historical records and set up one reporting model with the same request, project, type, and site fields for everyone. That way a KPI meant the same thing in every report.
What changed
Reports went from 2 to 5 days to under an hour. Recurring ad hoc requests dropped about 40%, because the answers were already on the dashboard.

API automation that ended the weekly copy-paste routine

Up to 20 hrs/week saved at peak

Same routine every week: export, clean, re-upload. So I had Python read and write the data through the API instead.

PythonREST APIAPI-driven automationData quality testing
Read the full storyShow less
Problem
Reporting depended on exporting data, cleaning it in Excel, and uploading it again. During busy weeks, that ate most of a workday.
What I did
I wrote Python scripts that read and update the data straight through the Smartsheet API, so there's no export step at all. I also added validation rules that flag bad entries instead of letting them slide through. That's what got people to stop double-checking by hand.
What changed
It saved up to 20 hours a week during peak periods, and the checks caught data errors before they showed up in reports.

Fixing ~400 equipment records after a reorg

~400 records reassigned

About 400 equipment records had the wrong owner after a reorg, and maintenance deadlines were still running.

Data quality remediationCross-system reconciliationData integrityMaximoAudit readiness
Read the full storyShow less
Problem
After the reorg, a big chunk of asset records in Maximo still pointed to the old owner or org. Left alone, that means missed maintenance and calibration, and eventually an audit finding.
What I did
I checked the records against the source data, worked through the corrections with the site admin, and traced mismatches across Maximo, MES, and JDE back to where they started. So we fixed the cause, not just the symptom.
What changed
Every record ended up with the right owner, so maintenance and calibration notices went to the people who actually had the equipment.

Controlled-access program across 3 sites

~50% faster turnaround

Access requests for a restricted program ran on email across the US, Singapore, and Costa Rica. I turned it into a workflow that keeps its own audit trail.

Requirements analysisWorkflow designApproval automationAccess governanceMulti-site implementation
Read the full storyShow less
Problem
Requests moved by email between 3 sites. There was no standard form, no way to see approval status, and when audits came, no clean record of who approved what.
What I did
I sat with the approvers at each site to see how it really worked, and every site had its own unwritten process. Then I redesigned the form with eligibility questions up front, set up routing by site and project type, wrote the process map and training, and rolled it out to all 3 sites.
What changed
Turnaround dropped about 50%. And access reviews became a report I could just run, instead of a two-week project.

Moving requests off paper and email

4 teams, 1 system

Requests came in on paper and by email. Details got missed, and nobody could say how much work was coming in.

Digital intakeWorkflow automationLifecycle trackingSmartsheetMulti-team adoption
Read the full storyShow less
Problem
Requests often showed up incomplete. Requesters couldn't see status, so we got constant "where's my order?" messages, and there wasn't any usable data to report workload from.
What I did
I built Smartsheet intake forms that capture everything up front, set up automatic confirmations and team alerts, added barcode and QR scanning to cut typing errors, and built the dashboards and monthly reports off the same data.
What changed
The first team ran 400+ requests and 22K+ samples a year through it. 3 more teams adopted the same setup, so 4 teams worked from one system with the same definitions. The status-chasing mostly stopped.

About

Most of my work starts the same way: a team runs on email, spreadsheets, and memory.

Requests get lost. Approvals sit in someone's inbox. And nobody can tell you how much work is in the queue without a few days of digging.

So I sit with the people doing the work, figure out what actually happens, and rebuild it into something that runs on its own: a clear intake form, approvals everyone can see, clean data, and a dashboard that answers the usual questions before anyone asks.

I did this for 9 years at an FDA-regulated medical device company, where every process had to hold up in an audit. That taught me to build the audit trail into the process instead of piecing it together later.

I have a B.S. in Statistics from UC Davis and a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt, and I'm finishing an M.S. in Data Analytics Engineering at Northeastern.
