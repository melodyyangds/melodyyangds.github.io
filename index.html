About

Most of my work starts the same way: a team has a process that runs on email, spreadsheets, and memory.

Requests get lost. Approvals happen in someone's inbox. Nobody can answer the question "how much work do we actually have right now?" — at least not without three days of digging.

What I do is sit with the people doing the work, map what actually happens rather than what the procedure says it does, and rebuild it as a real system. Structured intake. Routing and approval that's visible. Clean data underneath. A dashboard that answers the recurring question before anyone has to ask it.

I did that for nine years at Edwards Lifesciences in Class III medical device operations, where a workflow also has to hold up to an ISO 13485 audit — which changes how you design it. The trail has to be a by-product of the process, not something you assemble afterward.

I'm completing a Master of Science in Data Analytics Engineering at Northeastern University. My undergraduate degree is a Bachelor of Science in Statistics from the University of California, Davis, and I hold a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt.

Selected work

Reporting rebuild: from a five-day wait to same day

~40% fewer ad hoc requests

Every metrics question turned into a manual pull, and the numbers didn't always agree with each other.

Problem
There was no governed reporting layer. Answering a leadership question took 2–5 business days of manual work, and because the logic was rebuilt per report, two people could pull the same metric and get different numbers.
Approach
I designed an ETL and auto-refresh process over 6K+ historical records. I rebuilt the reporting model around consistent request, project, type, and site dimensions, so KPI definitions were standardized instead of recreated in each report.
Outcome
Reporting turnaround went from 2–5 days to under one day, and recurring ad hoc requests fell roughly 40% because the answers were already on screen.

API automation that ended the weekly export-and-paste cycle

Up to 20 hrs/week saved at peak

The same export, clean, and re-upload routine every week — until it read and wrote directly through the API instead.

Problem
Reporting depended on manually exporting operational data, cleaning it in Excel, and re-uploading it. During peak demand that consumed most of a workday, every week.
Approach
I wrote Python automation that read and updated the data directly through the Smartsheet API, removing the export step entirely. The part that made it stick was the validation layer — rules that flag inconsistent entries rather than passing them through silently, which is what got people to stop double-checking by hand.
Outcome
Saved up to 20 hours per week during peak demand periods, and the built-in checks caught data errors that used to surface downstream.

~400 records corrected before audit

Roughly 400 equipment records carrying the wrong owner, with a compliance clock running.

Controlled-access program, rebuilt across three countries

~50% faster turnaround

An access request process that ran on email across US, Singapore, and Costa Rica — redesigned as a governed workflow that produces its own audit evidence.

Problem
Requests for a restricted program moved by email across three sites. There was no standard intake, no visible approval status, and when the audit came, no clean record of who approved what or why.
Approach
I sat with the approvers at each site and mapped what actually happened — each site had a different unwritten path and none of it was documented. Then I redesigned the intake with eligibility questions up front, built routing by site and project type, wrote the process map and training, and rolled it out across all three regions.
Outcome
Turnaround dropped roughly 50%. The bigger change was that access reviews became a report I could run instead of a two-week reconstruction project.

Digital intake replacing paper and email requests

Four functions, one system

High-volume operational requests arrived by paper work order and email. Requirements got missed, and nobody could report on demand.

Problem
Intake was unstructured, so requests routinely arrived incomplete. Status was invisible to the requester, which meant a steady stream of "where is my order?" interruptions — and there was no usable data to report workload from.
Approach
I built Smartsheet intake forms that captured every requirement at submission, set up automated confirmations and team notifications, added barcode and QR scanning to cut manual entry error, and built the dashboards and monthly management reporting on top of the same data.
Outcome
400+ requests and 22K+ samples a year in tissue processing alone. The same intake pattern was then extended to lab testing, mechanical testing, and histology — four functions, each run by a different team, working from one system and one set of definitions. Status chasing largely stopped.

Finding where the rework was actually coming from

~$90K annual savings

Everyone had a theory about the source of rework. A full DMAIC project measured it instead.

Problem
Rework was consuming a meaningful share of team capacity, but the cause was assumed rather than measured — so previous fixes had targeted the wrong step.
Approach
I ran a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt project end to end: stakeholder analysis, voice of the customer, process mapping, Pareto analysis, Gemba walk, fishbone and cause-and-effect matrix, FMEA, and control charts to establish what was actually driving the variation.
Outcome
Identified the real drivers, quantified the improvement, and delivered approximately $90K in annual savings.
